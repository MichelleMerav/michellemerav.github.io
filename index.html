About Me:

I am a bioengineering researcher and artist interested in how we design materials to interact with the human body. Growing up, I carried a sketchbook everywhere and spent my time drawing and sketching. That early love for visual art ultimately led me to materials, design, and medical applications.

I completed my double major in Biochemistry and Studio Art at Hunter College, where I brought my artistic background directly into my lab work. Right now, I am pursuing my master's in Biomedical Engineering at Columbia University as a CUNY Pathways Fellow. My focus is mostly on polymer hydrogel synthesis, live-cell imaging, tumor microenvironment modeling, and drug delivery.

In the lab, I work on making nanoparticle hydrogels and targeted delivery systems to control local cellular environments. I rely on techniques like confocal fluorescence, TEM, and DIC microscopy to see cell motility and physical changes in 3D. Outside of research, I use my background in ceramics and fine arts to demonstrate spatial design and help make scientific concepts easier to conceptualize and understand.

Whether I am working with clay in the studio or building hydrogels at the bench, I love bringing creativity into research to build better biomedical tools and make science more accessible.
