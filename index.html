About Me:

Michal Merav is a bioengineering researcher and artist interested in how we design materials to interact with the human body. Growing up, she carried a sketchbook everywhere and spent her time drawing and sketching. That early love for visual art ultimately led her to materials, design, and medical applications.

She completed her double major in Biochemistry and Studio Art at Hunter College, where she brought her artistic background directly into her lab work. Right now, she is pursuing her master's in Biomedical Engineering at Columbia University as a CUNY Pathways Fellow. Her focus is mostly on polymer hydrogel synthesis, live-cell imaging, tumor microenvironment modeling, and drug delivery.

In the lab, she works on making nanoparticle hydrogels and targeted delivery systems to control local cellular environments. She relies on techniques like confocal fluorescence, TEM, and DIC microscopy to see cell motility and physical changes in 3D. Outside of research, she uses her background in ceramics and fine arts to demonstrate spatial design and help make scientific concepts easier to conceptualize and understand.

Whether she is working with clay in the studio or building hydrogels at the bench, she loves bringing creativity into research to build better biomedical tools and make science more accessible.
